## Article 11 (Watching and anti-cheat (Aegis))

1. Aegis is the anti-cheat built into PocketRoles. It is a feature for keeping a host's room fair and protecting the people who join, and it is part of our unofficial MOD. It does not belong to Innersloth. This article says **what Aegis does and what it does not do**. The levels of detection, room restrictions, shared bans and appeals are written in the Play Rules (Articles 2 to 5 and Articles 8 to 10) (Article 15). How records are handled is written in the Privacy Policy (3.9 to 3.11, Article 9 and Article 12) (Article 24).
2. What Aegis does
   1. **The scan before launching** (StarPocket Client, today's launcher and the tray Aegis): before PocketRoles starts, it checks a set list of things on the host's PC. How many things are checked differs by version and by which app is used (Client, launcher or tray). Each thing checked is shown as one line on the scan screen. Only the following are checked.
      - The names and contents (hashes) of the files in the game and MOD folders: whether PocketRoles.dll has changed since it was put there, whether the BepInEx `plugins` folder has any plugin other than PocketRoles, whether there are DLLs used by cheats or swap-in DLLs next to Among Us.exe, and whether BepInEx is installed
      - The **names** and **executable files** of running apps: they are compared, inside this PC, with the list of tools that can be used for cheating. Where an executable file is is only asked of Windows; the memory, the windows and the screens of apps are not looked at. Only apps in the same Windows session as the host are looked at (checking executable files starts with v0.5.5)
      - Windows' protection settings (Secure Boot, TPM, test mode, the vulnerable driver blocklist and so on): only the values of Windows settings that anyone can read are read
      - The signature and version of the definitions file, the versions of Among Us and PocketRoles, the Aegis items in the MOD settings (whether detection is on), and the number of lines in the list the host writes themselves: these are only reported, and they stop nothing

      When something that does not meet Play Rules, Articles 3 and 4 (codes starting with R) is found, the Play button stops and the reason and a code are shown. **Fix it and you can play right away.** This is not a limit under Article 13 (Article 13(4)). When the checks happen is written in Privacy Policy 3.11.
   2. **Watching inside the game**
      - PocketRoles' self-check: at start-up, every 60 seconds, and when the menu is opened, it checks the names, places and signatures of the DLLs loaded into the game, whether the game's code (GameAssembly.dll in memory) has been changed, and the names of the loaded drivers. It only checks inside the game itself. If something does not match, the next lobby cannot be made (Play Rules, Article 4(1)).
      - Checks inside the room: PocketRoles on the host's PC watches what the people who joined the host's room do in the game (kills, vents, abilities, tasks, how fast they move, messages, chat flooding, hurtful words and so on; Play Rules, Articles 2 and 5) and sorts it into the 3 levels "Certain", "Repeat" and "Notice" (Play Rules, Article 8). The numbers used for these checks are published in the definitions file.
      - The tray Aegis and StarPocket Client: while the game is running, they read the MOD's game log (`BepInEx\LogOutput.log`) and show a Windows notification when Aegis inside the game removes someone from a room or detects something. They also check the names and executable files of running apps every 30 seconds (in StarPocket Client, while the window is visible) and, if a tool that can be used for cheating is found, tell you and write it in `events.log` (they stop nothing).
   3. **Receiving the definitions file**: we receive from GitHub the file we signed, which contains the numbers used for checks, the names of tools that can be used for cheating, the NG words, the central lists (the shared-ban list, the erase list and the planned lift list) and the PocketRoles version needed. **It is only received; nothing about this PC is sent** (GitHub receives an IP address, as with any normal connection). Only files whose signature matches are used. When they are received and how to stop it are written in Article 12 and Privacy Policy 3.9.
   4. Hosts can turn off Aegis detection and use of the shared-ban list in the settings. Do not say "Aegis protects this room" in a room where you turned them off (Play Rules, Article 7(1)(7)).
3. What Aegis does not look at
   1. It does not look at photos or documents, other apps' screens, key presses, browsers or passwords.
   2. It does not read or write the memory of other apps (including Among Us). It does not look at their module lists, windows or screens. For other games too, it only asks Windows where the executable file is and reads that file from the disk; it never puts anything into those games.
   3. It does not open personal files outside the game and MOD folders.
   4. It does not install drivers. It does not install services that stay running. It does not restart the PC and does not change Windows settings.
   5. It does not collect PC part numbers (hardware IDs). It does not use them for limits either (Article 13(4)).
   6. It puts nothing on, and checks nothing on, the PCs and phones of people who only join a room (Privacy Policy Article 9(1)). Checks inside the room are made only from the game's messages that reach the host's PC.
   7. Normal conversation is not kept in Aegis's records (Play Rules, Article 5(6)). IP addresses are not kept either.
4. Where it runs. What is not sent, and what is sent
   1. Aegis runs **only inside the host's PC.** No part of it runs on our servers. The records on the host's PC never reach us. We cannot look at the records on other hosts' PCs.
   2. **What is never sent**
      - The results of the PC scan. Even when you press "Contact support" on the "You can't play yet" screen, what is filled in first is only the code, the app version and the language. The names of what was found are not included (users write them if they want to).
      - The results of the self-check inside the game
      - The results of comparing with the shared-ban list (paragraph 6)
      - Evidence records and the room-restriction list. These reach us only when a host makes a "report zip" or a "One player's evidence" zip themselves and sends it by email (Privacy Policy 3.3). Nothing is sent automatically.
   3. **What is sent** (sent from the host's PC straight to the other side; it does not reach us)
      - The connection that receives the definitions file (GitHub; it is only received)
      - Official reports to Innersloth: for a person removed from a room for a "Certain" cheat found in the room, the reason and who they are are sent to Innersloth (at most once every 30 days for the same person). This is not the result of the PC scan; it is a check made inside the room. It will be off at first and sent only when the host turns it on, on StarPocket Client's first screen or in the settings (today's build has it on by default, so it is sent unless you turn it off; we will fix this before release).
      - Chat translation and the Discord webhook are not Aegis features. What they send and how to stop them are written in Privacy Policy 3.9.
   4. Also checking the shared-ban list with our server is not done now. If we ever start it, nothing would be sent: the whole list would be received from our server and compared inside this PC (nothing about your account is sent; Privacy Policy 3.9). If we start it, we will ask you to press "Agree" again, as in Article 26(4). The PCs of people who have not agreed do not make this connection.
5. How long records stay
   1. Aegis keeps an evidence record on the host's PC when it removes someone from a room and when it restricts them from the room. What is in it (the name, a hash of the PUID, the code for erasing records, the time, the room, the rule, the numbers, 20 lines of that person's log and so on) is written in Privacy Policy 3.10. IP addresses and other people's chat are not included.
 2. Evidence records are kept for **90 days** for everyone, whether or not they were restricted from a room. This is because appeals can arrive late. A record pointed at by a room restriction that is still running stays until 90 days after it was made or 30 days after the restriction ends, whichever is later (it stays for good when there is no end date). Only the 2 lines of the game log that back up the evidence are kept with the record.
   3. The game log, Aegis's record of events (`events.log`), and the report zip and "One player's evidence" zip on the desktop are deleted after 30 days.
   4. The room-restriction list stays while the restriction lasts. Only the count of breaches goes back to 0 one year after the last restriction ends.
   5. If you want your records erased, type `/cmd id` in a room's chat and send the code it shows to support (Play Rules, Article 11; Privacy Policy Article 12). We erase them without waiting for the 90 days. The smallest amount of information needed for a room restriction that is still running (the hash, the level and the end date), and the evidence record for that restriction, stay.
   6. We decide these defaults, but it is the host who holds the records. Hosts must not post records on social media or anywhere else (Play Rules, Article 7(1)(4)).
6. Comparing with the shared-ban list
   1. PocketRoles inside the game compares a hash of the PUID of a person who joined the room with the shared-ban list in the definitions file it received, **inside the host's PC. Nothing is sent.** Names and friend codes are not on the list (shared bans start with v0.5.5).
   2. PocketRoles inside the game also checks, with the same definitions file and inside the host's PC, whether the Among Us account that is signed in is on the shared-ban list (this is planned for v0.5.5; Play Rules, Article 9(6)). It only turns the PUID and the friend code into a hash of the same form as the list and compares them; the PUID and the friend code are used only in memory and are never written down or sent. Nothing new is sent for this.
   3. The result of the check on your own account only stays in a small file on the host's PC (`access-status.txt`; planned). It is not sent anywhere and does not go in the report zip (Privacy Policy 3.10).
   4. Only the author can make a shared ban, and it is decided by looking at Aegis records (evidence). Not being able to create rooms while a shared ban lasts, how it is lifted, and how to appeal are written in Article 15(5) and Play Rules, Articles 9 and 10.
